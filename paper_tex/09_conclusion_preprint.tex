\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A belief has a state, a transition, and a likelihood, and this survey asked of every included system which of the three it has and where each comes from. The answer is uneven. The field has given the agent a state, from the trace to stores, filters, and world models, and a transition, with credit made denser at every level below the belief; it has built more than twenty ways to measure belief without a shared one. The likelihood is the term still supplied from outside: the representations that can be falsified are not the ones the agent can reason about, the mechanisms that revise beliefs do not route the error to the claim that produced it, and the two methods that score the belief with the environment's own returns score a binary belief and stand alone. The next step is precise: a written belief that carries uncertainty, is scored by the agent's own matured predictions, and receives the error at the claim that made it, evaluated where the latent state is known.
