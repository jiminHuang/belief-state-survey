\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A memory records what has happened, whereas a belief is a hypothesis about what is true, open to testing and revision by future evidence. This survey asked of every system which of the belief's three parts it has and where each comes from. The field has given the agent a state, from the trace to stores, filters, and world models, a revision, with credit made denser at every level below the belief, and more than twenty ways to measure belief without a shared one. The test that turns a record into a hypothesis is still supplied from outside: testable representations are not the ones the agent can reason about, revision does not route the error to the claim that produced it, and the two methods that train a written belief on the agent's own observations use a binary one. The next step is a written belief that carries uncertainty, is scored by the agent's own matured predictions, receives the error at the claim that made it, and is evaluated where the latent state is known.
