\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This survey took the belief state apart into its state, transition, and likelihood terms and asked of 462 papers which terms a system has and where each comes from. The field has given the agent a state, from the trace to stores, filters, and world models, and a transition, with credit made denser at every level below the state; it has built more than twenty ways to measure belief without a shared one, and the likelihood is still supplied from outside. Three findings carry across the axes. The representations that can be falsified are not the ones the agent can reason about; the mechanisms that revise beliefs do not route the error to the claim that produced it; and the methods that credit the belief at all borrow their likelihood from an oracle the environment does not supply. The next step is precise: an agent whose own matured predictions supply the likelihood for its written belief, evaluated where the latent state is known. The taxonomy, screening record, and benchmark catalogue are released to make that step measurable.\label{endconcl}
