Language-model agents now act over horizons of hours or months, and the failures reported from these settings are failures of state, not of reasoning. A model that solves a problem in one pass loses much of its accuracy when the same information arrives over several turns \citep{laban2025lost}; an agent given only observations wins a fraction of the episodes won by one that reads the simulator's latent state \citep{alaswad2026why}; and in markets stronger backbones do not reliably produce stronger trading agents \citep{xie2025finben,li2024investorbench}. What these failures share is that the task required the agent to hold something between decisions, a belief about what is true, how certain it is, and what would change it, and the agent never formed it, never tested it, or revised it without acting on the revision.

Decision theory has a name for that something. Under partial observability an agent acts not on the world but on a belief about it \citep{astrom1965optimal,kaelbling1998planning}, and the belief is what a Bayes filter maintains \citep{thrun2005probabilistic}. The name matters because it comes with a structure: a belief has a \emph{state} that says what is believed and how firmly, a \emph{transition} that carries it across an action, and a \emph{likelihood} that says how the next observation scores it \citep{thrun2005probabilistic,friston2010free}; belief-revision theory adds that changing it must not discard the rest \citep{alchourron1985logic}. This is the shift the survey argues for: a memory records what has happened, whereas a belief is a hypothesis about what is true of the world, and is therefore open to testing and revision by future evidence. The line runs through memory research, not around it: a store that attaches a confidence and an update rule to its claims already has the first two parts \citep{zhang2024memory,luo2026storage}, and what makes it a belief is the third, the likelihood that connects the hypothesis to the evidence that can support or falsify it. Memory research gave the agent a state, moving it from the reasoning trace \citep{wei2022chain,yao2023react} into stores that persist, reflect, and abstract experience \citep{park2023generative,shinn2023reflexion}, and uncertainty research gave that state a confidence \citep{lin2022teaching,kadavath2022language,lin2026uncertainty}. Post-training gave it a transition, teaching agents to overwrite a written state \citep{zhou2025mem1,yu2025memagent} while rewarding the finished response \citep{ouyang2022training}, the reasoning step \citep{lightman2023lets,zheng2025prm}, or the action \citep{zhou2024archer,zhang2025landscape,zhang2026reasoning}. The likelihood, which lets the agent's own observations correct what it believes, is still supplied from outside, by a fixed observation model, a simulator, or a teacher.

Work on the belief itself exists, from dialogue state tracking \citep{lee2021dialogue} to agents that write beliefs about tasks, opponents, and markets \citep{kim2024qube,cross2024hypothetical,yu2024fincon} and benchmarks that score the belief rather than its outcome \citep{xu2026should,deb2026stocktake,chao2026stale}, but it rarely cites itself (Figure~\ref{fig:timeline}) and no survey takes it as its object: memory surveys organise by what is stored, reward and credit surveys stop at the action, uncertainty surveys score an output rather than a state, and evaluation surveys report no belief-level metric (Section~\ref{sec:related}, Table~\ref{tab:surveys}). This survey takes the belief state as its object and sorts the literature by which of its three parts a system has and where each comes from: written by the model, fixed by the designer, learned, or absent (Figure~\ref{fig:taxonomy}), and follows the belief around the cycle of Figure~\ref{fig:loop}: Section~\ref{sec:representation} asks where the state lives and what form it takes; Section~\ref{sec:revision} how it is carried and revised, and where revision fails; Sections~\ref{sec:learning}--\ref{sec:evaluation} who scores the belief against evidence, whether the score becomes a learning signal and \emph{which object receives it}, and how evaluation measures each part. We cover language-model agents acting under partial observability, 2020 to September 2026, collected by a scripted protocol (Appendix~\ref{app:method}); Section~\ref{sec:challenges} states the open problems, chief among them a belief that carries uncertainty and is tested against the agent's own observations.
