\section{Revision and Its Failures}
\label{sec:revision}

Bayesian filtering and active inference agree on how a belief should change. It predicts, evidence arrives, and the mismatch drives the update \citep{friston2010free,friston2017active}, routed by factor graphs to the factors that produced the prediction \citep{kschischang2001factor,loeliger2004introduction}. In Equation~\ref{eq:filter} the transition carries the belief forward and the likelihood decides how far the observation moves it. The language-model literature imported the vocabulary but rarely the routing, appending feedback to the context or folding it into self-critique loops \citep{huang2022inner,madaan2023self,kim2023language}.

\subsection{Four Failure Modes}
\label{sec:rev:modes}

\ifnature\input{rev_failures}\else
Contextual Belief Management names three ways a belief can fail on new input, a \emph{failed stay} in which it moves without evidence, a \emph{failed update} in which it survives evidence against it, and a \emph{failed isolation} in which irrelevant input contaminates it \citep{xu2026should}. Failed update is also the base model's default \citep{sharma2023towards,geng2025accumulating,zhang2025large}, and in consolidated memory a quarter or more of the agent's own speculations resurface as user-stated facts \citep{verbovoy2026rank}. A fourth, the \emph{failed act}, revises the belief but leaves the policy unconditioned on it \citep{deb2026stocktake,gao2026auditing}. Table~\ref{tab:failures} and Fig.~\ref{fig:loop} place them, and Appendix~\ref{app:revdetail} gives the evidence for each.
\fi

\subsection{Mechanisms of Revision}
\label{sec:rev:mech}

\ifnature\input{rev_mechanisms}\else
The mechanisms that respond to failed update fall into three families by what triggers them, \emph{contradiction} among stored claims \citep{wang2026toki,hao2026memops,xu2026governed,qin2026mom}, a check \emph{before commitment} by self-audit, a parametric veto, semantic entropy, or resampling \citep{yuan2026verify,song2026agent,yan2026denoiseflow,wang2026t2po}, which cannot catch a belief that is consistent and wrong, and \emph{surprise} at a returned observation \citep{song2026d,zhang2026selfevolvingx}, the only trigger of the three that sees a later observation. FinCon sends each revision only to the agents that need it, routing the error to an agent though not yet to a claim \citep{yu2024fincon}.
\fi

\insight{2}{\textbf{Revision is triggered, not routed.} Contradiction, self-audit, and surprise decide \emph{when} a belief changes, but none decides \emph{which claim} the error belongs to. Until the error is routed to the claim that made the prediction, agents will keep repairing memories while beliefs stay wrong.}
