The mechanisms that respond to failed update fall into three families by what triggers them. The first is triggered by \emph{contradiction} among stored claims and resolves it with operator algebras, first-class update and forget operations, or supersession contracts \citep{wang2026toki,hao2026memops,xu2026governed,qin2026mom}. The second is triggered \emph{before commitment}, by self-audit, a parametric veto, semantic entropy, or resampling \citep{yuan2026verify,song2026agent,yan2026denoiseflow,wang2026t2po}. These see no future observation and so cannot catch a belief that is consistent and wrong. The third is triggered by \emph{surprise}. Reward-prediction error routes writes to a fast or a slow path \citep{song2026d}, and prediction--observation mismatches become semantic rules \citep{zhang2026selfevolvingx}. FinCon revises its investment beliefs after each episode and sends each revision only to the agents that need it, routing the error to an agent though not yet to a claim \citep{yu2024fincon}.
