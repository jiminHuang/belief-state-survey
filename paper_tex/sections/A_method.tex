\section{Paper Collection Protocol}
\label{app:method}

The search is scripted and rerunnable; the configuration (including every query string and term list), raw hits, per-stage counts, and the full-text coding record for the runs reported here (identifiers \texttt{20260915\_v1} for 2025--2026 and \texttt{20260916\_v3} for the 2020--2026 window) are released with the paper.

\paragraph{Queries.}
Twenty-three keyword queries were defined in the first run, four to five per survey section; a second run added eleven queries on evidence acquisition and theory-of-mind belief modelling (34 in total): representation (Section~\ref{sec:representation}), revision (Section~\ref{sec:revision}), learning (Section~\ref{sec:learning}), evidence (Section~\ref{sec:evidence}), and evaluation (Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}).

\paragraph{Sources.}
Source A queried OpenAlex across all venues with publication date from 1~January 2025, 50 results per query. Source B queried OpenAlex restricted to the arXiv source with publication date from 1~January 2026, 50 results per query, to improve recall of preprints. The arXiv API itself was rate-limited during the run; the OpenAlex mirror of arXiv lags by a few days.

\paragraph{Screening.}
Records were deduplicated by identifier and then by normalised title, so that a preprint and its journal version count once. Rule-based screening on title and abstract applied one inclusion criterion for an LLM-related term (I1), one exclusion criterion for off-domain applications (E1), and one inclusion criterion requiring at least two topic terms from a fixed list (I2). Records without an abstract of at least 200 characters were excluded. Included records were ranked by a relevance score used only for ordering the manual full-text screen, never for inclusion.

\paragraph{Second and third runs.}
The second run (34 queries, same sources and rules) returned 834 unique records, 338 after screening, of which 96 were published in 2026; only two of those had not already been read in the first run, which we take as evidence that the first run had saturated the keyword neighbourhood. The third run extended the window to 1~January 2020 with 100 results per query and returned 4{,}471 unique records, 3{,}999 after title deduplication and 2{,}079 after rule screening. Keyword ranking is dominated by generic high-citation papers before 2025, so the 2020--2025 foundations were enumerated as a curated list of 90 titles resolved through OpenAlex for venue and citation data (Table~\ref{tab:masterfull}); 75 of them were read in full and 73 included.

\paragraph{Citation-tree expansion.}
Keyword search cannot reach papers that describe belief maintenance in other words, so the included set was expanded by snowballing over the citation graph. Each round took the papers newly included in the previous round as seeds, fetched their reference lists (backward) and the papers citing them (forward) from the Semantic Scholar Graph API, kept candidates published from 2020, removed everything already screened, and applied the same rule-based screen as the keyword runs. Because forward citation of recent preprints is dense, the rule-included candidates were sampled for full-text reading by a fixed rule (topic score $\geq 10$, or the word \emph{belief} in title or abstract, or linked to at least three seeds, or a world-model paper from 2025 with score $\geq 7$), and every sampled paper was read and coded with the same fields as the keyword runs. Round one was seeded by the 179 keyword-included papers and each later round by the papers the previous round added; the full-text sample shrank from 302 to 115, 83, and 26 across four rounds, and the expansion stopped after the round whose sample fell below thirty, the stopping rule fixed in advance (Table~\ref{tab:snowball}).

\paragraph{Fifth round, targeted.}
After the four rounds above, we found a method the protocol had not reached (ReBel; \citealp{tang2026rewarding}), posted after most of our seeds and cited by almost no one yet. We treated it as a seed for a fifth, targeted round: the Semantic Scholar batch endpoint was rate-limited throughout, so we used ReBel's own reference list (46 entries), an OpenAlex search on belief-consistency and belief-level supervision (85 candidates), and a Hugging Face Papers search on nine related phrases (743 candidates, 211 after the rule screen and deduplication). Full-text screening added 23 papers (3 peer-reviewed), of which four score a belief or a prediction against the agent's own observations (two score a written belief: ReBel and ABBEL); every paper whose signal source mattered to a claim was re-read in full. One of the 23 (PABU) had passed the rule screen in the third keyword run but was never sampled, which is the protocol's own miss. The additions are marked R5 in the released record and in Table~\ref{tab:years}.

\paragraph{Sixth round.}
A final targeted round added four papers found after the fifth round, two posted after the keyword and citation runs, Jev-Mem and REFLEX \citep{jiang2026jevmem,wu2026reflex}, and two those runs did not reach, FLAG-TRADER and Conv-FinRe \citep{xiong2025flagtrader,wang2026convfinre}. They were read in full, coded with the same fields, and are counted in the totals, the sensitivity analysis, and the master table (column R6 of Table~\ref{tab:years}).

\paragraph{Update run.}
A final update run repeated the OpenAlex and Hugging Face Papers queries and rule screen for September 2026. It returned 1{,}051 unique records and 434 rule-screened candidates, of which 47 were read in full and 29 included (3 peer-reviewed): among them two surveys that formalise a Bayes-filter belief for one setting \citep{cai2026complex,tang2026proactive} and three methods that credit a probabilistic belief against a known answer \citep{zhang2026toward,wang2026gavel,hu2026infinite}. They are counted throughout (column U1 of Table~\ref{tab:years}), and no insight changes direction (Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity}).

\paragraph{Year distribution.}
Table~\ref{tab:years} gives the included papers by year, source, and whether peer-reviewed. The share that is peer-reviewed falls with recency, from four in five before 2025 to about one in nine for 2026, which is the ordinary lag of review rather than a property of the field.

\paragraph{Evidence level.}
Of the 517 papers included after full-text reading and the author check (178 from keyword search, 283 from citation-tree expansion, 23 from the targeted fifth round, 4 from the sixth, and 29 from the update run), 181 are peer-reviewed and 336 are preprints; every number quoted in the text is the paper's own headline figure, taken from the abstract or the reported table, and none has been reproduced by us.

\paragraph{Full-text screening.}
A paper passed full-text screening if it (a) concerns a language-model agent or a component used by one, and (b) constructs, revises, tests, learns, or evaluates an explicit state carried between decisions, or documents a failure of such a state; benchmarks and surveys that do (b) were kept and coded as such. Each paper read in full was recorded with the fields: who maintains the belief (source of the belief state), credited object, revision signal (source of the revision and test), whether belief-level metrics are reported, survey section, and role. The record is the source of Fig.~\ref{fig:taxonomy}, Tables~\ref{tab:representation}--\ref{tab:benchmarks}, and Fig.~\ref{fig:metrics}. Screening and coding were carried out with large-language-model agents: each sampled paper was read and coded against these fields under the fixed codebook above, and every paper whose coding supports a claim in the text was re-read in full. The authors defined the framework, the inclusion criteria, and the codebook, and are responsible for the record. An author then checked every coding by hand: of 2{,}590 field values on 518 papers, four were changed, one paper (a survey) was excluded and two belief-maintainer codes were corrected, giving agreement of 99.8\% on inclusion, 99.6\% on who maintains the belief, and 100\% on credited object, revision signal, and metric. A system counts as testing a written belief against the agent's own observations only if the model writes the belief and the signal that scores it is computed from observations the agent itself receives, with no oracle, simulator state, or teacher; the record's \texttt{own\_obs\_test} column lists the three that qualify, ReBel, ABBEL, and the pre-language-model GATA. The record has not been re-coded by a second, independent annotator.
