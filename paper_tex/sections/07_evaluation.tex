\section{The Likelihood Term as a Metric: Evaluating Belief Apart from Outcome}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\input{fig_metrics}

A correct answer can follow from a correct belief, a wrong belief that did not matter, or a belief revised too late, and outcome metrics cannot separate the three, which is why stronger backbones do not yield stronger trading agents \citep{xie2025finben,li2024investorbench}. Earlier agent benchmarks score task success or recall \citep{shridhar2020alfworld,liu2023agentbench,yao2024taubench}; recent work added belief-level metrics, each defining its own; each measures one term from outside (calibration the state, accuracy and revision the transition, Bayesian coherence the likelihood, the belief--action gap whether the policy uses any). Fig.~\ref{fig:metrics} aligns them by construct; Table~\ref{tab:benchmarks} in the appendix catalogues the benchmarks.

\subsection{Constructs}
\label{sec:eval:constructs}

\emph{Calibration over a trajectory} has the most statistics and the least comparability: checkpoint and episode calibration, posterior entropy, and proper scoring rules on time-gated forecasts express one construct four ways, none computed on another paper's system \citep{lin2026uncertainty,singh2026agent,chattopadhayay2026belief,dixon2026belief,ghosh2026finbench}. \emph{Belief accuracy and revision} are measured against a known answer in a closed world, after a minimal premise edit, and across recall, inference, and change detection \citep{xu2026should,dhanda2026deltalogic,nguyen2026dynamic}, against human annotation \citep{myakala2026beliefshift,chao2026stale,hao2026memops,yang2026groupmembench}, a probed spatial map or a screenplay's state \citep{zhang2026theory,tian2026stage}, and theory-of-mind benchmarks \citep{kim2023fantom,bawatneh2026omnitom,wang2026mindgames}. \emph{Memory validity} is scored per operation or by penalising invalidated memories \citep{chen2025halumem,wu2024longmemeval,liu2026worldmemarena,uddin2026recall}. \emph{Bayesian coherence} compares a belief trajectory with an ideal updater: total-variation distance to the exact posterior \citep{samanta2026bayesbench}, a martingale test on successive beliefs \citep{he2025martingale}, and the correlation between the model's update and the log-odds of the evidence \citep{imran2025are,yuan2025do}. The \emph{belief--action gap} is the youngest construct: STOCKTAKE scores the agent against an exact Bayes filter on the same observations \citep{deb2026stocktake}, the Werewolf audit reports action--belief consistency \citep{gao2026auditing}, SimpleToM separates inferring a belief from applying it \citep{gu2024simpletom}, and Agent-ToM monitors beliefs from the trajectory \citep{ahmed2026agenttom}.

Every construct beyond calibration needs a latent state to compare against: controlled environments provide it \citep{deb2026stocktake,xu2026should,alaswad2026why}, long-horizon simulators know it but report belief through proxies \citep{he2026ycbench,zhang2026retailbenchx,han2026can}, and historical replay provides neither \citep{herculean2026}. 

\insight{5}{\textbf{Belief cannot be scored by outcome.} Seven constructs, more than twenty metrics, almost no cross-paper comparison, and only the fair oracle scores belief and action on the same episodes. Progress on belief will be measurable only where the latent state is known, so controlled environments, not replay, are where the next results should come from.}
