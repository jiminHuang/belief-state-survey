\begin{abstract}
Language-model agents now plan, call tools, and act over many steps, and the failures that dominate reports from such settings are failures of state rather than of reasoning: the agent loses track of what it has learned, keeps acting on contradicted evidence, or revises what it believes without changing what it does. What such an agent must hold between decisions is, in the language of partially observed decision-making, a belief, and a belief has three parts: a state that says what is believed and how firmly, a transition that carries it across an action, and a likelihood that says how the next observation scores it. This survey takes the belief state as its object and sorts 462 papers from 2020 to 2026 by which of the three parts a system has and where each comes from: written by the model, fixed by the designer, learned, or absent. Memory and uncertainty research gave the agent a state, revision and post-training gave it a transition, and the likelihood, the part that lets the agent's own observations correct what it believes, is supplied from outside wherever it exists. We follow the three parts through construction, revision, and testing, align the fragmented belief-level evaluation literature by construct, catalogue 33 benchmarks, and identify the gap the alignment exposes: no method in the corpus learns a likelihood for a written belief from the agent's own observations.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Limitations}
This survey covers work indexed by OpenAlex and arXiv from January 2020 to 15 September 2026 and retrieved by the keyword protocol and four rounds of citation-tree expansion in Appendix~\ref{app:method}; papers that describe belief maintenance without using any of our search terms may be missed. Our taxonomy assigns each method one source per term, although several systems combine sources; the codings are ours and were not independently replicated. Appendix~\ref{app:method} reports how each insight fares on the peer-reviewed subset alone. The survey reports numbers as stated by the cited papers and does not re-run any method; headline figures from preprints may change in later versions. Finance is used as the running example because delayed and partially observed evidence is the norm there; the taxonomy itself is domain-agnostic, but we have not validated it against embodied or dialogue agents in the same depth.
