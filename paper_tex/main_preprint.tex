\begin{abstract}
Language-model agents now act over many steps, and their failures are failures of state rather than of reasoning. They lose track of what they have learned, keep acting on contradicted evidence, or revise a belief without changing what they do. What an agent must hold between decisions is not a memory of what happened but a testable belief, a hypothesis that later evidence can test and revise. It has three parts, a state that says what is believed and how firmly, a revision that carries it across an action, and a test that scores it against the next observation. This survey sorts 518 papers by which parts a system has and where each comes from. Memory and uncertainty research supplied the state and post-training the revision, but the test, which lets the agent's own observations correct its belief, is still supplied from outside. Aligning belief-level evaluation by construct exposes the gap. The first systems to test a written belief against the agent's own observations use a binary belief, work in isolation, and lack a framework that connects representation, testability, revision, credit, and belief--action consistency.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Limitations}
This survey covers work indexed by OpenAlex and arXiv from January 2020 to 30 September 2026, retrieved by the keyword protocol, citation-tree expansion, two targeted rounds, and the first monthly update (Appendix~\ref{app:method}); papers that describe belief maintenance without using any of our search terms may be missed. Our taxonomy assigns each method one source per part, although several systems combine sources; the codings were produced with large-language-model assistance under our codebook (Appendix~\ref{app:method}) and have not been independently replicated. Appendix~\ref{app:method} reports how each insight fares on the peer-reviewed subset alone. One directly relevant method reached us through a reader's comment rather than the protocol; Appendix~\ref{app:method} reports the targeted search that followed. The survey reports numbers as stated by the cited papers and does not re-run any method; headline figures from preprints may change in later versions. Finance is used as the running example because delayed and partially observed evidence is the norm there; the taxonomy itself is domain-agnostic, but we have not validated it against embodied or dialogue agents in the same depth.
