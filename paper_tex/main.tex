\author{Anonymous ARR submission}

\begin{abstract}
Language-model agents now act over many steps, and their failures are failures of state rather than of reasoning. What an agent must hold between decisions is not a memory of what happened but a testable belief, a hypothesis that later evidence can support or falsify. We define such a belief by three parts, a state that says what is believed and how firmly, a revision that carries it across an action, and a test that scores it against the next observation, and sort 518 papers from 2020 to September 2026 by which parts a system has and where each comes from. Memory and uncertainty research supplied the state and post-training the revision, but the test is still supplied from outside. Of the 297 systems that train anything, 50 credit the belief state, 35 of them in 2026, and only two test a written belief against the agent's own observations, both with a binary belief. More than twenty belief-level metrics, aligned by construct, share almost no evaluated system. Every finding holds on the 181 peer-reviewed papers alone, and we release the taxonomy, screening record, and benchmark catalogue.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Limitations}
This survey covers work indexed by OpenAlex and arXiv from January 2020 to 30 September 2026, retrieved by the keyword protocol, citation-tree expansion, two targeted rounds, and an update run (Appendix~\ref{app:method}); papers that describe belief maintenance without using any of our search terms may be missed. Our taxonomy assigns each method one source per part, although several systems combine sources; the codings were produced with large-language-model assistance under our codebook (Appendix~\ref{app:method}) and have not been independently replicated. Appendix~\ref{app:method} reports how each insight fares on the peer-reviewed subset alone. One directly relevant method, ReBel, appeared after most of our seeds and was found only after the main protocol, and Appendix~\ref{app:method} reports the targeted search it prompted. The survey reports numbers as stated by the cited papers and does not re-run any method; headline figures from preprints may change in later versions. Finance is used as the running example because delayed and partially observed evidence is the norm there; the taxonomy itself is domain-agnostic, but we have not validated it against embodied or dialogue agents in the same depth.
